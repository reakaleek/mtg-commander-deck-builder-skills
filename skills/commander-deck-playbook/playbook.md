# Playbook structure

Fill each section from the submitted list and the interview. Do not prescribe turn numbers or category quotas. Do not name cards here.

## Deck identity

What resource does this deck create, convert, and spend to win?

State the plan in plain language before listing lines.

## Mulligans

Which opening-hand roles does this list actually need?

Keep or mulligan from those roles, not from a generic hand recipe.

## Sequencing

Describe early, middle, and late play in terms of setup, conversion, and close.

Do not assign those stages to numbered turns.

## Commander timing

When does casting the commander advance the plan?

When does it expose the commander to removal or tax without enough payoff?

## Synergy packages

Group pieces that work together.

Explain how their Oracle texts interact. Separate a locked line from a nice overlap.

## Combo and win lines

Write each close as ordered steps.

Include prerequisites, what the table can interrupt, and what you still have after a stop.

Tutors only appear in a line if the deck contains the cards they would find.

## Pivot and recovery

What do you do after removal, a wipe, graveyard hate, commander tax, or a stopped win attempt?

Name the remaining engines in this list. Do not invent a backup that is not in the file.

## Threat assessment and politics

Use the playgroup they described.

Who do you need to answer, and when does this deck want to look smaller than it is?

## Rules reminders

Call out triggers, stack order, replacement effects, and timing only where this list's Oracle text makes them easy to miss.

## Common mistakes

Piloting errors specific to this list.

Skip generic Commander advice that would apply to any deck.

## Quick reference

A compact at-table card: keep, sequence, protect, close, recover.

Keep it short enough to glance at between turns.
